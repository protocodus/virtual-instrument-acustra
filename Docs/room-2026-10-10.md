### The acoustics, and where each number comes from

- **Geometry.** The room (5.2 × 4.1 × 2.7 m), the image sources and their levels are the existing ones (decision of 2026-10-01). The guitar's position follows from the existing delays: the floor image at 2.025 m puts the microphones at 1.05 m height; the walls behind the player and the microphones at 3.30 and 4.91 m put them 1.49 and 2.61 m away (sum 4.1 m); the side walls at 4.61 and 5.81 m put them 2.30 and 2.90 m away (sum 5.2 m).
- **ITD.** The near-field pair is two omnis 0.20 m apart along the guitar's long axis (evaluation finding 11). The lag is 0.20 m × the arrival's direction cosine along that axis / 343 m/s: left wall 0.9974, floor then left wall 0.9152, right wall 0.9983, floor then right wall 0.9441. The 1/r level difference between the microphones (≤ 0.4 dB) is ignored.
- **Mixing time.** Polack (1993): t_mix ≈ √V ms, 7.6 ms for 57.6 m³. The late field's first arrivals start at 9 ms and are spread one line per millisecond to 25 ms, where the first recirculation begins (8 + 17.3 + 1 ms).
- **Coherence.** Cook et al., JASA 27 (1955): a diffuse field's coherence between two omnis at spacing d is sin(kd)/kd. Octave-band averages of the target are 0.963/0.856/0.508/−0.085 at 125/250/500/1000 Hz. The first-order shape fits 125–500 Hz (fitted corner 540–555 Hz). It cannot reproduce the negative lobe near 1.2 kHz, so it overstates 1 kHz coherence by about 0.3.
- **Which microphone feeds which reflection.** A plate mode with a nodal line between the two microphones radiates as a dipole along their axis. It sends nothing towards the median plane (floor, ceiling, front, back) and opposite phases towards the two side walls. The mid is therefore physically right for the first group, and each side wall gets its nearer microphone. This is an approximation: it ignores the near-field-to-far-field relation of the dipole term.
- **Modal overlap.** Schroeder: the Rayleigh statistics of a room's response need overlap ≳ 3. The real room's Schroeder frequency is about 180 Hz, with overlap about 4 at 200 Hz. The 8-line FDN had about 1.1 at every frequency, so its narrowband peaks were heavier-tailed than a real room's. Sixteen lines give about 2.2.
- **Pattern choice (an engineering selection, disclosed).** A Sylvester–Hadamard row used as an output pattern reads one line back through the mix (H·r_k = 16·e_k) wherever the readings fall within a period of each other. With rows 4/11 the two outputs' third-octave powers differed by −5.5 to +9.7 dB, which breaks the coherence split's assumption of equal-power, uncorrelated outputs. An off-tree replica of the late field screened 51 row/line-order combinations, and 40 random non-Hadamard patterns did no better. The chosen combination is X = row 1, Y = row 14, mid on row 13, side on row 2, with the line lengths scrambled over the indices. At 48 kHz its third-octave power ratio is 1.8 dB rms and the outputs' |r| at most 0.25 (100 Hz–2 kHz). In the engine its X/Y third-octave ratios are within ±1.8 dB except 354 Hz (−4.4 dB) and 1 kHz (+3.1 dB). The criterion is the model's own assumption, not any recording.

## CPU and memory

The paired protocol of [cpu-optimization-2026-10-05.md](cpu-optimization-2026-10-05.md):
A and B are compiled into one executable in separate namespaces (`-Dacustra=acustra_baseline/current`),
with identical flags (GCC 13.3, `-std=c++20 -O3 -DNDEBUG`). Both play the same
authored event stream continuously. After 200 ms of untimed preroll, callback
order alternates and only `process()` is timed; outputs are checked finite.
48 kHz, 128 frames, 5 rounds × 2250 callbacks per scenario, pinned to one core
(`taskset -c 3`), three runs. The machine was shared with other agents (load
average about 2.0), so absolute times are noisy; the paired ratios are the
robust figure. Medians of the three run medians, µs per callback (deadline 2667 µs):

| Scenario | A p50 | B p50 | A p95 | B p95 | A p99 | B p99 | paired B/A, p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Room 0.5, strummed phrase (chords every 0.4 s, melody) | 175.1 | 180.8 | 280.1 | 293.1 | 353.0 | 370.0 | 1.034 |
| Room 0.5, held six-string chord | 138.9 | 143.3 | 235.3 | 245.9 | 281.4 | 301.3 | 1.032 |
| Room 0.5, chord released, room ringing out | 139.9 | 145.1 | 214.4 | 228.1 | 269.4 | 289.5 | 1.036 |
| Room 0 control, held chord | 125.9 | 124.0 | 173.4 | 175.6 | 219.6 | 220.0 | 0.984 |

With Room on, the engine costs 3.2–3.6% more per callback (about +5–6 µs at
128 frames, 48 kHz). The room alone, timed on noise, went from 48–51 to
71–74 ns per host sample at 48 kHz; restructuring the 16-line field around one
masked write position brought it down from 127 ns. With Room 0 the cost is
unchanged: the code path is the same, and the 1.6% difference in the control is
layout noise. `sizeof(RoomAmbience)` rises from 147,848 to 295,504 bytes, and
`sizeof(AcustraEngine)` from 3.76 to 3.91 MB (extended layout 14.24 to 14.39 MB);
the engine is heap-allocated everywhere already. No allocation, lock or file
I/O was added. The room's stores wrap by masking, with a `static_assert` on
power-of-two capacities.

## Finding 14 and missed phenomenon 9

**Room as distance.** Not restructured. Moving the virtual microphone with Room
would also have to move the direct sound and the reflections' timing. The direct
sound is the calibrated near-field capture (the construction loudness tables are
measured with Room off), and moving delays under a sounding room would need
crossfaded tap sets. Room stays a send: the level of the room at the pair's own
position. The engine's and the plug-in's comments now say so instead of "a
microphone well out in it". README's wording (the same phrase) is left to the
integrator.

**Small-room low modes.** Not implemented. The room's axial modes would sit at
33, 42, 64, 66, 84, 99, 125–132 Hz, near E2 (82.4 Hz), G2 (98 Hz) and B2/C3.
Nothing here can show such modes "measurably help": no recording of this room
exists to fit their levels and damping. The coherent low field already carries
realistic single-position modal fluctuation (std 4.5–4.8 dB), and the per-note
sweep shows that the boom risk on particular notes is exactly what the
16-line change had to contain.

## Limits and what needs listening

- **Tail versus early sound.** With the late share kept at 1.5, B moves energy
  from after 50 ms into 9–50 ms. Relative to the guitar, on average material B's
  tail after 80 ms is about 2 dB weaker than A's, and its first 25 ms about
  2 dB stronger. Within the room the shift is −2.9/+1.3 dB; the room's 0.5–1 dB
  rise on average material offsets part of it. Listen for whether B sounds
  drier or less roomy at the same setting. If it does, the follow-up is `roomLateShare`: 1.5 → 2.9 keeps A's
  tail-to-reflection ratio. The diffuse-field estimate for a 0.32 m position in
  this room (Eyring decay of the image-source density) puts the energy from 9 ms
  at about 4 times the ten strongest reflections', at their physical levels.
- **Bass image.** The room's low end is now one coherent field at both
  microphones, as it is for a real spaced pair. Its bass no longer
  sounds wide; any single note's room level follows one realisation of the
  room's modes (F♯4, D4 and C4 are B's loudest-room notes, 7.7, 8.1 and
  8.1 dB under the guitar; A's were C♯4, B3 and A♯3, at 7.5, 8.4 and 8.8).
- **Restored mid/high content.** Partials the pair's mid cancels (around
  1–2 kHz on the Dreadnought, where L/R correlate at −0.4) now ring in the room.
  The room is about 1 dB louder above 300 Hz on average. Listen for brightness
  or "ring" in the room at 1–2 kHz, especially at Room 1.0.
- **Side reflections** are placed by time (0.53–0.58 ms) instead of level.
  Listen for image stability on headphones.
- **Mono capture** now hears the room at its own (right) position: early
  reflections 3 dB lower than before, about 0.4–0.7 dB less room overall.
